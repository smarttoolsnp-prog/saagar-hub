📋 Who can donate blood

  • Age 18 to 60, and generally in good health.
  • Weight of at least 50 kg for men and roughly 45–50 kg for women.
  • At least 3 months since your last donation (men often can give every 3 months, women every 4).
  • Haemoglobin in the normal range — the technician checks this with a quick finger-prick test.
  • No fever, cold, infection or antibiotics in the last week; no tattoo or piercing in the last 6 months.

A healthy adult has around 5 litres of blood and a donation takes about 350–450 ml. Your body replaces the plasma within a day or two and the red cells over a few weeks.

🇳🇵 Finding a blood bank in Nepal

The quickest route in an emergency is almost always a phone call, not a search. Try, in this order:

  1. Ask the hospital where the patient is admitted. Most hospitals with a blood bank will arrange units internally and call donors for you.
  2. Contact Nepal Red Cross Society — they run the largest network of blood banks across the districts. Search “Nepal Red Cross blood bank + your district” for the current number.
  3. Post in a local blood-donor group. District-level Facebook groups and “Blood Donors Nepal” style communities respond fast, and donors often come to the hospital directly.
  4. Check with the nearest teaching hospital (for example the larger hospitals in Kathmandu, Pokhara, Chitwan and Biratnagar) — they usually have the widest stock of rarer groups.

Keeping a small card with your own blood group, allergies and an emergency contact in your wallet genuinely helps when you cannot speak for yourself.
